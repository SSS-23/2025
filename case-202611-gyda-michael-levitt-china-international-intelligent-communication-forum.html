GYDA Advances Multilateral Engagement as Nobel Laureate Michael Levitt Delivers Keynote at the 2026 China International Intelligent Communication Forum

Location

Wuxi, Jiangsu Province, China

Date

Nov 9, 2026

The 2026 China International Intelligent Communication Forum, jointly hosted by China Media Group (CMG) and the Jiangsu Provincial People's Government, was held in Wuxi, Jiangsu Province. Michael Levitt, the 2013 Nobel Laureate in Chemistry, a member of the U.S. National Academy of Sciences, and Senior Advisor to the Global Youth Development Alliance (GYDA), attended the forum and delivered a keynote address titled "Many Intelligences, One Conversation."

Under the theme "New Ecosystem, New Opportunities, New Future," the forum produced a series of outcomes, including the Wuxi Initiative of the China International Intelligent Communication Forum. For GYDA, the occasion provided an opportunity to connect scientific expertise, national media platforms, and local industrial ecosystems with international cooperation on youth development and digital inclusion.

1. Multilateral Participation: Connecting Institutions, Research, Media, and Industry

Yu Junsheng, Vice President of China Media Group, and Xu Ying, Member of the Standing Committee of the CPC Jiangsu Provincial Committee and Head of the Provincial Publicity Department, delivered opening remarks on behalf of the hosts.

Oleg Kopylov, Deputy Secretary-General of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization; Zamir Ahmed Awan, Founding Chairman of the Global Silk Route Research Alliance (GSRRA); and Cai Jianfeng, Deputy Secretary of the CPC Wuxi Municipal Committee and Mayor of Wuxi, also attended and addressed the forum.

The keynote session brought together representatives from science, technology, media, and industry, including Lu Bingheng, Director of the National Additive Manufacturing Innovation Center and Professor at Xi'an Jiaotong University; Michael Levitt; Guo Tong, Chairman of CCTV.com and Chairman and General Manager of Yangshipin; Vikram Channa, Vice President, Head of Content, and Editor-in-Chief of Discovery Channel Greater China; Ge Yuefeng, Deputy General Manager of China Time and Space Information Group; and Scott Ross, Founder of Digital Domain.

The participation of an intergovernmental organization, an international research alliance, national media, local government, and industry representatives reflected the forum's cross-sector and multilateral character. The Wuxi Initiative, released in conjunction with the forum, called for technology for good, deeper international cooperation, and mutual learning among civilizations, establishing a shared framework for future engagement.

2. Michael Levitt's Keynote: Four Forms of Intelligence, Communication, and Trust

Drawing on his expertise in computational biology, Levitt identified four forms of intelligence that coexist in today's world: biological intelligence, cultural intelligence, individual intelligence, and machine intelligence. "Communication is the bond that connects them—and that is precisely what this forum is about," he said.

Reflecting on developments in his own field, Levitt observed that predicting protein structures from amino acid sequences had been a challenge for physics-based approaches for fifty years, while machine learning achieved major advances within five years, building on decades of experimental research and openly available data. He also pointed to the two Nobel Prizes awarded in 2024 for work related to artificial intelligence as indicators of this technological transformation.

Levitt connected these scientific advances with the opportunities presented by digital inclusion. He emphasized that AI is reducing barriers between languages and academic disciplines. Every slide of his keynote included Chinese text alongside English, prepared with the assistance of AI. As he observed, "almost anyone can now, for the first time, converse with anyone else in their own language."

As the costs of experimentation and learning from mistakes decline, more people can participate in asking questions, testing ideas, and evaluating answers. This perspective closely aligns with GYDA's commitment to digital inclusion, particularly the expansion of access to digital capabilities and multilingual communication.

Levitt also addressed the challenges arising from increasingly accessible content-generation technologies. When thousands of articles or videos can be produced within minutes, he argued, content becomes abundant while trust becomes increasingly valuable.

"Verification becomes a core skill," he stated, adding that "nothing is the truth until it is confirmed by evidence." He emphasized that education should develop curiosity, critical judgment, and the ability to verify information, rather than focus exclusively on the transmission of facts.

Looking toward 2056, Levitt envisioned a future in which young scientists collaborating with AI agents could accomplish in a single year what previous generations of researchers achieved over an entire career. He encouraged young people to question, test, and verify information, recognizing that AI systems will play an increasingly significant role throughout their lives.

3. GYDA's Role: Connecting Multilateral Dialogue with Youth Development

The forum brought together complementary institutional strengths. China Media Group and the Jiangsu Provincial People's Government provided the national media platform and public agenda. Intergovernmental organizations and research institutions contributed international perspectives, scientific evidence, and cross-border networks. Wuxi offered an urban and industrial environment in which emerging technologies and practical applications could be explored.

Within this broader ecosystem, GYDA's contribution focused on connecting scientific dialogue, institutional cooperation, and technological innovation with the international agenda for youth development. Rather than serving as an event organizer, GYDA seeks to strengthen the institutional links through which international expertise and cross-sector cooperation can contribute to youth opportunities and digital inclusion.

About GYDA

The Global Youth Development Alliance (GYDA) is a non-profit association legally registered in Paris, France, under the French Law of 1901. GYDA works to empower young people and strengthen institutional capacity through international cooperation, digital innovation, and cross-border engagement.

Its areas of focus include AI governance, digital youth leadership, regional innovation ecosystems, youth multiplier initiatives, and digital inclusion. Through international projects, institutional engagement, and collaboration with partners across multiple countries, GYDA seeks to expand opportunities for young people and contribute to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).
